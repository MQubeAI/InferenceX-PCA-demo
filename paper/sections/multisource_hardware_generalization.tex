\section{Capability-Based Hardware Representation}
InferenceX identifies accelerators categorically, which limits controlled study
of transfer across hardware. We joined accepted Epoch physical descriptors to a
row-count-preserving July operating-point view. Seven SKUs were accepted; H100
and H200 were excluded because shorthand labels do not establish exact variants.
On 7,168 rows and grouped configuration folds, identity-only, identity-plus-
capability, capability-only, and matched-precision variants achieved R$^2$ of
0.8377, 0.8382, 0.8375, and 0.8349, respectively. Thus descriptors did not
materially improve ordinary interpolation, but preserved predictive performance
without categorical identity. In leave-one-measured-SKU-out tests, capability RF
beat all tested training-only baselines in MAE. Transfer was uneven, particularly
for MI300X and MI355X; this is preliminary evidence for measured-SKU transfer,
not unreleased-hardware prediction or a calibrated extrapolation guarantee.
